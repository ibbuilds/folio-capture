# UI and internet aesthetics: 1980s–2026

The creator's [I.1 selection](selection-i1/README.md) supplies the versioned runtime vocabulary. The study below remains broader research context; its terminology does not override that selection. Independent semantic calibration remains pending.

Checked through **12 October 2026**. This investigation explains how to recognize and distinguish UI aesthetics when looking for specific inspiration. It covers historical interfaces, internet aesthetics, contemporary professional directions and surface treatments, including flat design. Historical styles can support excellent modern work; publication age is not a quality score.

Start with the [history and visual distinctions](ui-history.md), then use the [chronological reference](timeline.md) to follow the evolution from the 1980s to today. The [recognition guide](ui-evidence-review.md) gives visual traits, counterexamples, confusing neighbors, UI evidence and dating limits for every earlier concept, with additional historical profiles.

The expanded history reviews the original **68 concepts** and adds **34 historical interface-language/influence profiles**. These are research records, not 102 established aesthetics or a proposed list of filters. Dates distinguish artifact periods, releases, naming events and revivals. Cross-period directions remain separate when no defensible beginning is established.

The [additional source record](history-sources.md) contains 72 entries alongside the earlier 82-entry ledger. Access types distinguish focused reading, indexed excerpts, unavailable originals and static visual evidence. Eighteen additional frames/plates were inspected, alongside the earlier seven gallery previews. Some are later software renderings or concept illustrations; this is not a visual audit of every concept.

Machine-readable artifacts:

- [atlas.json](atlas.json): definitions, positive evidence, counterexamples, confusing neighbors, exact aliases, separate related search terms, approximate history, source IDs and proposed retrieval roles.
- [chronology.json](chronology.json): chronological research annotations, historical interface profiles, typed date events, UI connections and uncertainty. Approximate sorting boundaries are not invention years.
- [history-sources.json](history-sources.json): additional annotated evidence and access limits.
- [visual-checks.json](visual-checks.json): inspected frames, their scope, observations and distinctions.
- [research-sources.json](research-sources.json): annotated source ledger with publication/access information and claim limits.
- [sources.md](sources.md): linked source index.

The earlier [2000–2026 report](research.md), [concept index](concept-index.md) and [classification recommendations](classifier-recommendations.md) remain available. Their original review date is preserved; the expanded history supplies the later date checks and corrections.

This is **research only**. It does not choose the final allowed aesthetics or change the runtime taxonomy. No new Folio capture run, classifier run, design demo or library frontend was created. Published images were inspected as evidence; screenshots and historical art were not copied into the repo.
